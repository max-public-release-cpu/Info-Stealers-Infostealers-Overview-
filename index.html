What Is an Infostealer?

Info stealer malware quietly harvests passwords, cookies, and crypto wallets from an infected device — here's how it works, how it spreads, and how to defend against it.

Key takeaways

  • Infostealers silently collect passwords, browser data, session tokens, crypto wallets, and system information.
  • They commonly spread through cracked software, phishing emails, and malicious ads.
  • Stolen data is resold cheaply on underground markets and fuels identity theft and ransomware.
  • Patching, 2FA, and password hygiene are the most effective defenses.

Infostealer malware (also written "info stealer") is malicious software built to quietly harvest private data from a computer or phone and send it back to a cybercriminal. Infostealers are widely distributed as malware-as-a-service (MaaS), which lowers the technical bar and puts this capability in the hands of a broad range of attackers.

What data does an infostealer collect?

CategoryExamples
Login credentialsUsernames, passwords, and saved logins for email, social media, and banking
Browser dataSaved credit cards, search history, autofill entries, and cookies
Session tokensActive login keys that let attackers bypass 2FA and access accounts directly
Crypto walletsPrivate keys and digital currency funds
System informationDevice names, IP addresses, chat logs, and desktop screenshots

How does it spread?

  • Malicious downloads — fake game mods, cracked software, pirated applications
  • Phishing — deceptive emails and malicious links
  • Malvertising — harmful ads on otherwise legitimate websites

Why it's dangerous

Stolen data is often sold cheaply on underground markets, fueling identity theft, financial fraud, corporate network breaches, and ransomware attacks. Because session tokens can bypass 2FA entirely, a single infection can lead directly to account takeover even on well-protected accounts.

FAQ

Is an infostealer a virus?
Not exactly — it's a category of malware focused on data theft rather than self-replication, though it's often bundled with other payloads.
Can antivirus software detect infostealers?
Up-to-date antivirus with real-time protection catches many known variants, but new or customized strains can sometimes evade detection.
What should I do if I think I'm infected?
Disconnect from the internet, run a full scan, change passwords from a clean device, revoke active sessions, and enable 2FA.
